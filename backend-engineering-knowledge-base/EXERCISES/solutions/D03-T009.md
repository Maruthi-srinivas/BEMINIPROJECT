# Solutions — D03-T009

## Exercise 1

A critical section is the stretch of code that must not interleave with another actor on the same data. Synchronization is the mechanism that enforces that exclusion or that orders two events. The section should be short. The invariant is whatever must be true before and after it, not the lock itself.

The failure enters when a step is skipped or repeated without an invariant: a lock that is held across a remote call, or an early return that skips the unlock.

Mark that step on the diagram. If you cannot point to a step, the explanation is still a definition, not a mechanism.

## Exercise 2

The assertion should fail when a lock that is held across a remote call, or an early return that skips the unlock.

Watch lock hold time and the rate of lock-order waits. A test that only checks a 200 status does not lock the invariant.

## Exercise 3

Choose Critical sections and synchronization when a data invariant spans more than one write.

Avoid it when you can use an atomic structure or a single database statement instead.

A good answer names the metric that would change your mind: lock hold time and the rate of lock-order waits.
