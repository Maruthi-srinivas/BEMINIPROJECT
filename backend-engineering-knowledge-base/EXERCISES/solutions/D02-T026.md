# Solutions — D02-T026

## Exercise 1

Amortized analysis averages the cost of an operation over a sequence, so a rare expensive step can be charged to many cheap ones. Aggregate analysis divides total cost by the number of operations. The accounting method stores credit on objects. The potential method tracks a function of the whole structure. A dynamic array's doubling is the textbook case: most pushes are O(1), and the copy is paid for by the unused capacity.

The failure enters when a step is skipped or repeated without an invariant: using an amortized O(1) claim as a tail-latency SLO, then watching one resize stall a request.

Mark that step on the diagram. If you cannot point to a step, the explanation is still a definition, not a mechanism.

## Exercise 3

Choose Amortized analysis when you can tolerate an occasional expensive operation and you need a bound on the sequence.

Avoid it when a single call has a hard deadline, in which case you need the worst-case cost or a background flush.

A good answer names the metric that would change your mind: the latency of the rare expensive call, not only the average over a benchmark that hides it.
