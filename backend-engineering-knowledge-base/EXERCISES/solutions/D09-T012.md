# Solutions — D09-T012

## Exercise 1

An advisory lock is a lock on a key your application defines, not on a table row. PostgreSQL does not know what the key means. Session-level locks last until unlock or disconnect. Transaction-level locks last until commit or rollback. They coordinate application work such as a single worker claiming a job. They do not lock the rows the job will touch unless you also lock those rows.

The failure enters when a step is skipped or repeated without an invariant: two application nodes both thinking they hold the lock because one used a session lock and checked the wrong return value.

Mark that step on the diagram. If you cannot point to a step, the explanation is still a definition, not a mechanism.

## Exercise 3

Choose Advisory locks when you need a cooperative mutex and you do not have a natural row to lock.

Avoid it when you needed a row lock or a unique constraint, which actually protects the data.

A good answer names the metric that would change your mind: the fraction of workers who acquire the lock, and jobs that run twice.
