# Solutions — D14-T013

## Exercise 1

A dead-letter queue holds messages a consumer could not process after the retry policy ended, or that failed validation. It is a parking lot, not a trash can. Operators need the reason, the original payload, and a way to replay after a fix. A DLQ that nobody reads is a silent data loss. Poison messages should arrive there without stopping healthy traffic.

The failure enters when a step is skipped or repeated without an invariant: an unmonitored DLQ, or a replay that sends the same poison back in a loop.

Mark that step on the diagram. If you cannot point to a step, the explanation is still a definition, not a mechanism.

## Exercise 3

Choose Dead-letter queues (DLQs) when some messages will fail for a reason a retry cannot cure.

Avoid it when you drop them on the floor or you retry them forever on the main path.

A good answer names the metric that would change your mind: DLQ depth and age of the oldest dead-lettered message.
