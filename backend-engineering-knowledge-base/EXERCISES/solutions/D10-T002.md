# Solutions for D10-T002

## Exercise 1

Use `ratelimit:{tenantId}:user:{userId}:{yyyyMMddHHmm}` and `ratelimit:{tenantId}:ip:{ip}:{yyyyMMddHHmm}` with `INCR` and `EXPIRE` on first increment (TTL ~120s). Avoid `ratelimit:global`. Optionally hash IPs for GDPR.

## Exercise 2

Causes: TTL set to 3600s from login at top of hour causing synchronized expiry; or cron job calling `FLUSHDB`/`KEYS` cleanup. Confirm via `TTL session:...` samples and Redis `INFO`/`slowlog`, plus deploy timeline versus cron.

## Exercise 3

DynamoDB fits when you need durable conditional puts (`attribute_not_exists`) across regions with managed ops; Redis fits single-region low latency if AOF/replication and `SET NX` are acceptable and ops team knows Redis. Payments usually favor DynamoDB or SQL for durability evidence; Redis alone is weaker unless clustered with explicit fsync policy and runbooks.
