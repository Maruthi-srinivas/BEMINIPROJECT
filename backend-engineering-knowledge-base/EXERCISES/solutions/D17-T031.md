# Solutions — D17-T031

## Exercise 1

Real-time location tracking ingests frequent position updates and answers 'where is this entity now' and sometimes 'which entities are in this area.' The write rate is the design. A durable history is separate from the hot latest-point store. Geospatial indexes answer area queries. Clients interpolate between updates. Stale points must expire so a parked device does not look live.

The failure enters when a step is skipped or repeated without an invariant: writing every point through a transactional database at high frequency and falling behind, or an area query that scans every entity.

Mark that step on the diagram. If you cannot point to a step, the explanation is still a definition, not a mechanism.

## Exercise 3

Choose Real-time location tracking when the product needs current positions at a high write rate.

Avoid it when you store every raw point in the same transactional table you use for payments.

A good answer names the metric that would change your mind: ingest lag, points per second per partition, and area-query p99.
