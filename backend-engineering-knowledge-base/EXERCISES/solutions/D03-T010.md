# Solutions — D03-T010

## Exercise 1

A mutex gives exclusive ownership of a critical section to one holder. A semaphore counts permits and can allow a fixed number of concurrent entrants. A read-write lock allows either many readers or one writer. None of them chooses lock order for you. A mutex is not a semaphore you happened to initialize to one if the API allows an extra release.

The failure enters when a step is skipped or repeated without an invariant: a semaphore released once too often, or a writer starved by a continuous stream of readers.

Mark that step on the diagram. If you cannot point to a step, the explanation is still a definition, not a mechanism.

## Exercise 3

Choose Mutexes, semaphores, read-write locks when you need exclusion, a concurrency cap, or shared readers with rare writers.

Avoid it when the resource is already serialized by the database or by a single-threaded event loop.

A good answer names the metric that would change your mind: wait time per acquire, permit count, and writer starvation time.
