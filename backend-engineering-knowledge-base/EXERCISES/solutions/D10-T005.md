# Solutions — D10-T005

## Exercise 1

A graph database stores entities as vertices and relationships as edges, and it is built to traverse them. The cost follows the size of the traversed neighborhood, not a join of whole tables. A super-node with millions of edges turns a 'friend of friend' query into a scan. Graphs are a poor fit for bulk columnar analytics.

The failure enters when a step is skipped or repeated without an invariant: a traversal that touches a super-node and times out, or using a graph to replace a simple key lookup.

Mark that step on the diagram. If you cannot point to a step, the explanation is still a definition, not a mechanism.

## Exercise 3

Choose Graph databases when the product question is a relationship traversal of bounded depth.

Avoid it when the question is a key lookup or a large aggregation.
